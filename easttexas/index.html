East Texas Watch

Developers have discovered East Texas for the same reasons families love it: water, land and room. From Lufkin's old paper mill to the old plywood mill at Bon Wier, from Cedar Creek Lake to the Neches River, these projects share the same rivers and the same aquifer.

The projects

  • ProposedRecords

    Amp Z / Project Lufkin, Angelina County

    Former Southland Paper Mill on State Highway 103 East. About 1,041 acres, expandable to 4,000, targeting 245 MW and scaling to 2.1 GW. Asked the City of Lufkin for about 20 million gallons a day. See Angelina County Watch.

  • ProposedRecords

    HyperDataGrid and Longleaf, Angelina County

    A data center on Ben Dunn Road with five on-site wells, and WattBridge's 600 MW gas plant near Kurth Lake. ERCOT also lists a second 600 MW Longleaf plant and a 202 MW battery project.

  • Purchase agreementReporting, verify

    East Texas Energy Campus, Newton County

    About 1,600 acres at the former L-P Plywood mill in Bon Wier, on the Sabine River. Clean Holding Company signed a purchase agreement in July 2026; a tracker lists it at 1.8 GW.

  • AnnouncedReporting

    NextEra gas hub, Anderson County

    A $16 billion natural gas generation hub with a data center to follow. Residents fear for Lake Palestine and the Neches River.

  • WithdrawnReporting

    Diode Ventures, Henderson County

    Would have drawn up to 5 million gallons a day from Cedar Creek Lake. Withdrawn July 2026. See Henderson County Watch.

  • DeniedReporting

    Bitcoin mine near downtown Tyler, Smith County

    The City of Tyler denied the rezoning in July 2026.

  • ProposedReporting, verify

    Two projects near Texarkana, Bowie County

    An AI data center at the I-30 megasite in Hooks, and Project Big Pine at the TexAmericas Center.

Lessons for other counties

  • Old mill sites are targets

    They come with power lines, water rights, rail and industrial history. Watch every closed mill in your county.

  • Our rivers connect us

    The Angelina feeds the Neches. What happens upstream reaches everyone downstream, so neighboring counties should work together.

  • Water providers hold power

    Cedar Creek Lake was saved when the water district paused new commitments.

  • Use the Deep East Texas Standard

    Contract terms any county can ask for. See /standards.
