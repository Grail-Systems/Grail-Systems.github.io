Civic leadership pipeline · Angelina County

Every county seat in Angelina County, and how regular folks get one.

The county runs our roads, courts, jail, records and taxes. These seats decide how. This year, almost none of them had a real race.

Updated Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 · Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

15 of 16

county and precinct races on Angelina County's Nov. 3, 2026 ballot had only one name on it. The only contested county race was County Commissioner, Precinct 2. That's not a knock on anyone who serves. It means voters didn't get a choice, because nobody else stepped up.

You don't have to know everything. The state trains you.

A justice of the peace doesn't need a law degree. A new county commissioner doesn't need to have run a road crew. Texas requires training for these offices, so you learn the job by doing it. Every one of these seats also takes the state's free open-government training.

When each seat is on the ballot

County offices serve four-year terms. They're split into two groups, so some come up in 2028 and the rest in 2030.

On the ballot in November 2028

  • Sheriff
  • County Attorney
  • District Attorney, 159th District
  • Tax Assessor-Collector
  • County Commissioner, Precincts 1 and 3
  • Constable, Precincts 1, 2, 3 and 4

When to file: candidates for these races file in late 2027, ahead of the March 2028 party primaries. Get the exact dates from the Angelina County Elections Administration, 606 E. Lufkin Ave., or the Texas Secretary of State.

On the ballot in November 2030

  • County Judge
  • County Clerk
  • District Clerk
  • County Treasurer
  • Judges, County Courts at Law No. 1 and No. 2
  • County Commissioner, Precincts 2 and 4
  • Justice of the Peace, Precinct 1 (Places 1 and 2), and Precincts 2, 3 and 4

These were all on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot. Winners take office in January 2027.

What each seat does, and what it takes

To run for almost any county office, you need to be a U.S. citizen, at least 18, a Texas resident for 12 months, a resident of the county (or precinct) for 6 months, and a registered voter. A few offices add more, listed below.

OfficeWhat it doesExtra requirements and training
County JudgeLeads the Commissioners Court, the county's governing body, and plays a key role in the county budget.New judges take 30 hours of training in the first year, then 16 hours a year.
County CommissionerOne of four votes (plus the judge) on the Commissioners Court: budget, roads, tax rates, abatements and contracts.16 hours of training every year.
Justice of the PeaceHandles small claims, evictions, traffic tickets and other fine-only cases. Also serves as magistrate.No law degree required. 80 hours of training the first year, then 20 hours a year.
ConstableA licensed peace officer for the precinct. Serves court papers and backs up the justice of the peace.Must be eligible for a peace officer license and meet the state's education or experience rules. Regular training, including civil process.
SheriffRuns county law enforcement and the county jail.High school diploma or equivalent, plus an active peace officer license or eligibility for one. 40 hours of training every two years.
County ClerkKeeps the county's official records: deeds, marriages, court records and Commissioners Court minutes.20 hours of training a year.
District ClerkKeeps the records for the district courts.20 hours of training a year.
Tax Assessor-CollectorCollects property taxes and handles vehicle registration and titles.20 hours of training a year.
County TreasurerKeeps and accounts for the county's money.No extra requirements to run.
County Attorney and District AttorneyProsecute criminal cases and advise the county.Must be a licensed attorney.

Also elected

The Angelina County Appraisal District board (Places 1, 2 and 3) was on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot. That board oversees the district that sets the value of your property for taxes.
