Civic leadership pipeline · Angelina County

Every county seat in Angelina County, and how regular folks get one.

The county runs our roads, courts, jail, records and taxes. These seats decide how. This year, almost none of them had a real race. Here's what each seat really does, and how the state trains you for it.

Updated Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 · Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

15 of 16

county and precinct races on Angelina County's Nov. 3, 2026 ballot had only one name on it. The only contested county race was County Commissioner, Precinct 2. That's not a knock on anyone who serves. It means voters didn't get a choice, because nobody else stepped up.

You don't have to know everything. The state trains you.

  • Every new county official gets a free workshop. The Texas Association of Counties runs "Preparing to Take Office," free for newly elected judges, commissioners, treasurers, clerks, constables, justices of the peace, sheriffs, tax assessor-collectors, and county and district attorneys.
  • Each office has its own required training, every year, listed on each seat below. You keep learning the whole time you serve.
  • Every elected official takes the state's free Public Information Act training within 90 days. Commissioners and the county judge also take the Open Meetings Act training.
  • Most seats need no special degree. A justice of the peace doesn't need a law degree. A county judge doesn't need a law license. The few that do are marked.
  • These are paid jobs. The Commissioners Court sets county salaries in the county budget.

Who can run

For almost every county office: a U.S. citizen, at least 18, a Texas resident for 12 months, a resident of the county (or your precinct, for precinct offices) for 6 months, and a registered voter. A few offices add more, listed on each seat.

The Commissioners Court

County Commissioner 4 seats

What you'd actually doYou're one of five votes on the Commissioners Court, the county's governing body, alongside the county judge. Together you set the county budget and tax rate, oversee county roads and bridges, approve contracts, and vote on things like tax abatements and road-use agreements with big developers. Meetings are public.
Training you getAt least 16 hours every year, through courses certified by public universities. Plus the free Open Meetings and Public Information Act training.
Who can runThe basic requirements, and you must live in the precinct.
Next on the ballotPrecincts 1 and 3: November 2028. Precincts 2 and 4: November 2030.

County Judge

What you'd actually doLeads the Commissioners Court and plays a key role in the county budget. The county judge may also have some court duties.
Training you get30 hours of training in the first year, then 16 hours a year.
Who can runThe basic requirements. The Texas Constitution says the county judge must be "well informed in the law," but a law license is not required.
Next on the ballotNovember 2030.

Courts and law enforcement

Justice of the Peace 5 seats

What you'd actually doThe people's court. You handle small claims, evictions, traffic tickets and other fine-only cases, and you also serve as a magistrate. Many folks' only time in a courtroom is in front of a JP.
Training you getNo law degree required. 80 hours of training your first year, then 20 hours a year, mostly through the Texas Justice Courts Training Center.
Who can runThe basic requirements, and you must live in the precinct.
Next on the ballotPrecinct 1 (Places 1 and 2) and Precincts 2, 3 and 4: November 2030.

Constable 4 seats

What you'd actually doA licensed peace officer for your precinct. Constables serve court papers, support the justice of the peace, and have full law enforcement authority in the county.
Training you get40 hours of continuing education every two years, 20 of them in civil process.
Who can runThe basic requirements, plus you must be eligible for a peace officer license and meet the state's education or experience rules.
Next on the ballotAll four precincts: November 2028.

Sheriff

What you'd actually doRuns county law enforcement and the county jail.
Training you get40 hours of continuing education every two years.
Who can runThe basic requirements, plus a high school diploma or equivalent, and an active peace officer license or eligibility for one.
Next on the ballotNovember 2028.

County Attorney and District Attorney

What you'd actually doProsecute criminal cases for the people, and advise the county on legal matters.
Training you get15 hours of continuing education a year.
Who can runThe basic requirements, and you must be a licensed attorney.
Next on the ballotCounty Attorney and District Attorney (159th District): November 2028.

Judge, County Court at Law 2 seats

What you'd actually doHears civil and criminal cases that are bigger than a justice court handles.
Who can runAt least 25, a county resident for two years, and a licensed attorney who has practiced law or served as a judge in Texas for at least four years.
Next on the ballotCourts at Law No. 1 and No. 2: November 2030.

Records and money

County Clerk

What you'd actually doKeeps the county's official records: land deeds, marriage licenses, county court records and the minutes of the Commissioners Court. If you've ever looked up who owns a piece of land, you used this office.
Training you get20 hours a year.
Who can runThe basic requirements.
Next on the ballotNovember 2030.

District Clerk

What you'd actually doKeeps the records for the district courts.
Training you get20 hours a year.
Who can runThe basic requirements.
Next on the ballotNovember 2030.

Tax Assessor-Collector

What you'd actually doCollects property taxes and handles vehicle registration and titles.
Training you get20 hours before each anniversary of taking office.
Who can runThe basic requirements.
Next on the ballotNovember 2028.

County Treasurer

What you'd actually doThe county's banker. Keeps the county's money in its bank accounts and accounts for every dollar that comes in and goes out.
Training you getA required introductory course your first year, then 20 hours a year. The Texas Association of Counties runs a seminar just for new treasurers.
Who can runThe basic requirements.
Next on the ballotNovember 2030.

Also elected

Angelina County Appraisal District board

What you'd actually doOversee the appraisal district, the office that sets property values for taxes: its budget and its chief appraiser. The board doesn't set the value of any one person's property; the district's appraisers do.
Training you getThe state Comptroller publishes a director's manual and training video for board members.
On the ballotPlaces 1, 2 and 3 were on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot.

School boards, water supply corporations and other districts are coming next.

When to file

Candidates for the November 2028 county races file in late 2027, ahead of the March 2028 party primaries. Get the exact dates from the Angelina County Elections Administration, 606 E. Lufkin Ave., or the Texas Secretary of State.
