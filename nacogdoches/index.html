Nacogdoches County Watch

A California poultry company wants about a billion gallons of groundwater a year near Lake Nacogdoches. On Nov. 9 the groundwater board loosened the rule those wells would have to meet. Solar and battery projects are lining up, and a regional vote on how far our aquifers can drop happens at Nacogdoches City Hall on Nov. 18. Here’s what we know, where it comes from, and what you can do.

From Ground Truth Texas, by Grail Systems and the East Texas Legacy Coalition. Checked Nov. 11, 2026. Corrections welcome.

What’s happening

  • Pending

    Pitman Family Farms: five wells near Lake Nacogdoches

    Pitman Family Farms, a poultry producer based in Sanger, California, is seeking five wells for a poultry-processing plant on FM 225 near Lake Nacogdoches. The request is about 1 billion gallons a year, or roughly 2.7 to 3 million gallons a day, from deep Wilcox wells 1,900 to 2,300 feet down. The company says the project could create 1,000 jobs. Neighboring landowners have contested the permits, which are not approved. A preliminary hearing is expected later in November.

  • Nov. 9, 2026

    The rule changed after most of the crowd left

    The Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District board announced it would not vote on the Pitman permits, moved the rule changes to the end of the meeting, and approved them unanimously about an hour after most of the crowd had gone. Deep Wilcox wells, below 1,500 feet and pumping up to 4,000 acre-feet a year, no longer face the 50-foot drawdown cutbacks. Monitoring is still required. Read the full story →

  • Nov. 9, 2026

    The local water supplier spoke against it

    D&M Water Supply’s general manager, Charles Perkins, told the board his utility strongly opposes the Pitman permits, saying 3 million gallons a day is nearly twice D&M’s entire daily capacity, as reported by local news.

  • Nov. 10–11, 2026

    Both parties, one side

    Nacogdoches County Republican chair Brian Fitzgerald and Democratic chair John Watt are working together against the rule change. They plan to ask the Nacogdoches County Commissioners Court for an agenda item so residents can raise it there.

  • 2026

    Solar and battery projects

    More than 100 residents came to a Feb. 17 town hall about three proposed solar projects in rural Nacogdoches County. The City of Nacogdoches adopted zoning rules for battery storage in January, and a Florida company, ESA Solar, has shown interest in a 10-megawatt battery system on property inside the city. A summary of ERCOT’s August 2026 interconnection report lists five proposed projects in the county, about 1,003 megawatts: three solar and two battery storage.

Who decides

  • Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District

    Regulates groundwater in Angelina and Nacogdoches counties. 936-568-9292. Next board meeting: 1:30 PM Monday, Feb. 8, at Kurth Lake Lodge. Its board is appointed by the two counties and their cities.

  • Groundwater Management Area 11

    Four districts, including Pineywoods, that set how far aquifer levels may drop. Votes Nov. 18. Comments go to coordinator Teresa Griffin, tgriffin@pcgcd.org. Details →

  • Nacogdoches County Commissioners Court

    Appoints members of the groundwater board. Residents are asking for an agenda item on the rule change.
