What your group gets

The kit

A records-request builder

Your neighbors pick an agency and a topic, and the page writes a Texas Public Information Act request they can send in minutes. It works for any agency: county, city, groundwater district or state.

Deadline clocks

Under the Texas Public Information Act, an agency has 10 business days to hand over the records, tell you in writing when they’ll be ready, or ask the Attorney General for a ruling. Every request your group files gets a clock that skips weekends and state holidays, and turns red when an agency runs past it.

Your own County Watch page

Projects near you on the statewide map, your records and their status, aquifer water levels and EPA violations, all on one public page your neighbors, reporters and officials can check.

A records log

Every reply, letter and document an agency sends back gets logged by date on your page, the way Angelina’s requests are, so nothing gets lost between volunteers.

What stays free

The public record is never for sale

The statewide map, every county page, the request builder, the checklist and the Deep East Texas Standard stay free for everyone. What we charge for is our time and skill: setting up the kit for your group and keeping it running.

Our rules

  • We sell tools and skill, never sources. The records we find stay public.
  • We don’t take money from developers or the companies we track.
  • We never publish private residents’ names or contact information without permission.
  • If an outside tracker disagrees with the public record, the public record wins.
