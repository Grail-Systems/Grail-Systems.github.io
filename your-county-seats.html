Civic leadership pipeline · Any Texas county

Every local seat in your county, and how to find them.

We built this for Angelina County first. But every Texas county has the same kinds of seats, the same state training, and probably the same problem: a lot of races where only one name is on the ballot. Here's the map for anywhere in Texas.

Updated Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 · Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

What we found at home

In Angelina County in 2026: 15 of 16 county and precinct races on the November ballot had one name. In May, 11 school and college board seats had one name, Lufkin's Ward 5 and 6 races were canceled, and Diboll's whole city election was canceled. Your county may look the same. The only way to know is to look.

The seats every county has

County offices (elected, paid, four-year terms)

The Commissioners CourtA county judge and four commissioners set the county budget and tax rate, oversee roads and bridges, and approve contracts and tax abatements.
Courts and law enforcementJustices of the peace, constables, the sheriff, the county and district attorneys, and in many counties a county court at law.
Records and moneyCounty clerk, district clerk, tax assessor-collector and county treasurer.
Training you getThe Texas Association of Counties runs a "Preparing to Take Office" workshop, free for newly elected county officials, and each office has its own yearly training. A justice of the peace needs no law degree; a county judge needs no law license. A few seats, like county court at law judge, do require one.
Who can runFor almost every county office: a U.S. citizen, 18 or older, a Texas resident for 12 months, a resident of the county (or precinct) for 6 months, and a registered voter.
WhenNovember of even-numbered years, with party primaries in March. For the March 7, 2028 primaries, candidates file Nov. 13 – Dec. 13, 2027. You can also run as an independent: file a declaration of intent with the county judge in that same window, then a voter petition after the primary.

School boards (elected, unpaid)

What you'd doOversee the district, evaluate the superintendent, adopt the budget and tax rate, and decide whether to ask voters for a bond.
Training you getA step-by-step state program: local orientation, an orientation to the Texas Education Code, and training on student results, safety and more, much of it through the regional education service center. You can start some of it up to a year before you're elected.
Who can runU.S. citizen, 18 or older, registered voter, 12 months in Texas and 6 months in the district.

City councils (elected)

What you'd doPass the city budget, set the city tax rate and adopt local ordinances.
Training you getThe state's free online Open Meetings Act and Public Information Act courses, plus the Texas Municipal League's orientation for newly elected officials.
Who can runIn small general-law towns: U.S. citizen, 18 or older, registered voter, 12 months in Texas and 6 months in the city (12 months for mayor in "Type A" towns). Home-rule cities set some rules in their charter.
Plus city boardsPlanning and zoning, zoning appeals, parks and more. You apply, and the council appoints.

Water boards and special districts

Water supply corporationsMember-owned nonprofits. Any member 18 or older can run, and directors are elected at the yearly members' meeting, held between Jan. 1 and May 1. File at least 45 days before the meeting.
Utility and water districtsSpecial utility districts, water control and improvement districts and fresh water supply districts elect their directors in public elections.
Groundwater districts and river authoritiesSome are elected; some are appointed by commissioners courts, city councils or the Governor. Find out who appoints, and tell them you want the seat.

Before you file, anywhere in Texas

First formA Campaign Treasurer Appointment (Form CTA). Every candidate needs one, even if you won't raise or spend a dime. File it before you file for the ballot, raise or spend money, or announce.
Where it goesNot to Austin. City candidates file with the city secretary, school candidates with the school board secretary, and county and precinct candidates with the county clerk or elections administrator.
ReportsCampaign finance reports are due 30 days and 8 days before the election, plus Jan. 15 and July 15. Small campaigns can choose a lighter schedule on the CTA form.
Ads and signsAds must say "Political Advertising" (or "Pol. Adv.") and who paid for them. Keep signs off public road right-of-way, including poles and trees there.

Find every seat in your county in one evening

  1. Your county elections office website. Look for past election documents. "Order of cancellation" and "certification of unopposed candidates" tell you exactly which races had one name.
  2. The state Comptroller's county directory. Search "Comptroller county directory" and your county. It lists every taxing unit in the county: school districts, cities, the junior college and water districts. That's your list of elected boards.
  3. The Texas Water Development Board's county water sheet. It lists the "water user groups" in your county: the cities and water systems that supply it. That tells you which water boards are yours.
  4. Each school district's and city's election page. Board size, terms, which seats are up next, and the filing dates. Or call the city secretary or the superintendent's office.
  5. The Secretary of State's "Qualifications for All Public Offices" page. Who can run for what.
  6. Mark your calendar. May 1, 2027 local elections: filing runs Jan. 13 – Feb. 12, 2027; register to vote by Apr. 1; early voting Apr. 19–27. Nov. 2, 2027 elections: filing runs July 17 – Aug. 16, 2027. March 2028 primaries: filing runs Nov. 13 – Dec. 13, 2027.
