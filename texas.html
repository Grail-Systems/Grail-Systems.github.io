The big picture

The projects on this map are a fraction of what is coming. The More Than Just Parks tracker counts 466 data centers in Texas as of October 2026: 270 operating, 88 under construction and 108 proposed, each tied to a public source. Our map focuses on the large and contested projects; see their list for the full census.

  • The state has hit pause

    In August 2026 the Governor ordered an audit of every data center in the grid queue, after some ignored a state survey on water and power use. ERCOT paused approvals while the audit runs, expected to finish around December 10, 2026. In September he told TCEQ to suspend data center permits until the grid and water reviews are done.

  • Water is the blind spot

    Until this fall, Texas did not require data centers to report their water use, and the state water plan has not counted them. In September 2026 the Governor ordered the Water Development Board to enforce reporting, but the numbers are not public yet. That is why local agreements, and this map, matter. See every known number side by side.

  • Watch for savings claims without numbers

    Developers often say a design will save billions of gallons, without saying how much it will actually use, or where the water comes from. Ask for the number and the source, in writing.

  • Low-water designs exist

    Google's own reports show its Midlothian campus used 182.3 million gallons of water in 2024. Its air-cooled site in Pflugerville used about 10,000 gallons all year. When a developer says it can't avoid heavy water use, that's a choice, not a law of physics.

Sources: ERCOT and Governor's office figures as reported by the Texas Tribune and POWER Magazine; Houston Advanced Research Center white paper, January 2026; Texas Observer; Austin Chronicle.

      How to spot a project early

      Developers leave a paper trail long before they announce. Anyone can check these, for free:

      • State building filings (TDLR)

        Any big commercial building in Texas must be registered with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, with the owner, address, cost and start date. Search the TABS project database by county or city.

      • The sales tax exemption list (Comptroller)

        The Texas Comptroller publishes every data center registered for the state sales tax exemption, with owner and operator names. It is the same exemption the Governor wants repealed.

      • Air permits (TCEQ)

        Turbines and backup generators need air permits. A gas permit on rural land with nothing announced is a red flag.

      • Grid requests (ERCOT)

        ERCOT's large-load reports show how much new power is being requested, and where.

      • Land records (appraisal districts)

        Watch for large parcels bought by unfamiliar LLCs. Big companies often use shell names. Google built in Midlothian as Alamo Mission LLC.

      • Local agendas

        Commissioners court, city council, groundwater district and appraisal board agendas. Look for tax abatements, road closures, water contracts and nondisclosure agreements.

      • Company announcements

        Investor presentations, earnings calls and job postings often name a site before neighbors are told.
