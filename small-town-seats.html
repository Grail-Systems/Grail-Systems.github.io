Your town council: Hudson, Huntington, Diboll, Zavalla and Burke.

In a small town, a council seat can be won with a few dozen votes, or with no votes at all because nobody else filed. These are the people who set your city tax rate, pass the budget and decide what gets built. You can be one of them.

Updated Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 · Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

What happened in May 2026

  • Diboll: the whole city election was canceled. The mayor and the District 2, District 4 and At-Large Place 6 seats each had one candidate.
  • Hudson: voters had a real choice, and the numbers were small. The mayor's race was decided by 47 votes cast in all (36 to 11). The top two alderman candidates got 26 votes each.

One neighbor filing changes everything. A few dozen neighbors voting decides it.

City Council Member (every town)

Mayor or Council Member / Alderman

What you'd actually doThe council is the town's governing body. Together you pass the city's budget, set the city tax rate, adopt ordinances (local rules), and decide on things like streets, water and sewer service, police and parks. You meet in public, read the packet before each meeting, listen to your neighbors, and vote.
Training you getThe state's free online Open Meetings Act and Public Information Act courses within 90 days of taking office. The Texas Municipal League also runs an orientation made for newly elected city officials.
Before you fileTurn in a Campaign Treasurer Appointment (Form CTA) to the city secretary. Every candidate needs one, even if you won't raise or spend a dime. Register to vote by Apr. 1, 2027; early voting runs Apr. 19–27.
Who can run (small "general law" towns)
  • A U.S. citizen, 18 or older, and a registered voter in the city.
  • A Texas resident for 12 months.
  • A city resident for 6 months. In "Type A" towns, the mayor must have lived in the city for 12 months.
  • No final felony conviction (unless pardoned or released from the resulting disabilities).
No degree, no experience in office, no party.
TermTwo years in most small general-law towns, staggered so about half the seats come up each year.

Town by town

Diboll

The councilA mayor and six council members: four elected from districts (1 to 4) and two at large (Places 5 and 6). Diboll has its own home-rule charter.
Who can runUnder the city's posted rules: a U.S. citizen, 18 or older, a qualified Diboll voter for at least six months when you file, and a resident of Texas and of the city for at least one year before the election.
Next electionMay 1, 2027: District 1, District 3 and At-Large Place 5. May 2028: Mayor, District 2, District 4 and At-Large Place 6. In 2026 all four seats had one name.
AskCity Secretary, (936) 829-6802, City Hall, 400 Kenley.

Huntington

The councilA mayor and five council members.
Who can runThe city's posted rules: for mayor, one year in Texas before the filing deadline and one year in the city before election day; for council member, one year in Texas and six months in the city before the filing deadline. Age 18 and a registered voter in the city.
Next electionMay 1, 2027: three council seats. May 2028: the mayor and two council seats.
AskCity Hall, (936) 422-4195, 802 U.S. 69, Huntington.

Hudson

The councilA mayor and aldermen. In 2026 the mayor's office and two council seats were on the ballot.
What 2026 showedEvery race was contested, and the winning totals were 36 votes for mayor and 26 votes for each of the two council seats. During early voting, a machine problem let six voters choose only one council candidate; the county swapped in new machines.
AskHudson City Hall, 3458 Ted Trout Drive.

Zavalla and Burke

The councilBoth are small towns with a mayor and a council. The county's 2026 election page has no Zavalla city documents and only a filing notice for Burke, so the best way to find out which seats are up in May 2027 is to call or stop by City Hall in January.
Why it mattersSmall councils make big calls. In June 2026 Zavalla's council voted down an agreement for regular sheriff's patrols, leaving the town with no formal police service.
