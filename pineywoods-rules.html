Pineywoods Rule 5.15: verified facts

East Texas Legacy Coalition · Last checked Nov. 19, 2026, against the rules the district adopted Nov. 9, 2026

This page is our reference for the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District's large-well rule. Every statement below is checked against the source named under it. If we find an error, we fix it here and note the date.

The adopted rule

On Nov. 9, 2026, the district's board amended its rules. The adopted rules were posted on the district's website on Nov. 12 as "District Rules Effective November 9, 2026."

Source: pgcd.org/rules · adopted rules (PDF)

The cutbacks for big wells (Rule 5.15(a)–(c))

  • A well or group of wells permitted after March 23, 2026, for 1,500 to 4,000 acre-feet a year must have one monitoring well. Above 4,000, one more monitoring well is required for every 3,000 acre-feet a year.
  • If the average water level in the monitoring wells falls 50 feet below the starting level for three months in a row, the district must temporarily suspend or reduce the permit until the level recovers.
  • If it falls 75 feet below the starting level, the district must suspend pumping for the rest of the permit period.

Source: adopted Rule 5.15(a), (b) and (c)

The deep Wilcox exemption (Rule 5.15(d))

"If the permitted production is in the Wilcox Aquifer and all of the sands screened are below a depth of 1,500 feet, then (b) and (c), above, apply only when the total permitted production for the entity is 4,000 acre-feet/year or more. The monitoring wells required in (a) above apply to these wells."

In plain terms: Wilcox wells screened entirely below 1,500 feet don't have to cut back at 50 feet or shut off at 75 feet, as long as the permit holder's total stays under 4,000 acre-feet a year. They still need monitoring wells.

The rule says nothing about water quality. The exemption depends only on depth and volume. The district's own rules define brackish water as groundwater with at least 3,000 milligrams per liter of dissolved solids, and Rule 5.15 does not use that term.

Source: adopted Rule 5.15(d) and Rule 1.1(aa)

Other parts of the Nov. 9 rules

  • City water utilities are still exempt. "This rule does not apply to a retail public water utility if the water is used to provide for its service needs within its service area within the District." (Rule 5.15(i))
  • Data centers and power plants need monitoring wells. Permits for power generation and data or computing centers require monitoring wells with automated equipment that reports water levels directly to the district. (Rule 5.16)

The vote

The district's general manager confirmed in writing that the board adopted the amendments as written in the draft, and that "the vote was unanimous, minus Kevin Gee, who was not present for the meeting."

Source: email from General Manager Wil Blough to ETLC, Nov. 10, 2026

The Pitman Family Farms applications

At the Nov. 9 meeting, Pitman Family Farms' presentation described wells targeting 1,900 to 2,300 feet and a request of 3,363 acre-feet a year.

Lufkin's seat on the Pineywoods board

State law gives the Lufkin City Council one of the district's seven seats. This is who has held it, from the City's and the district's own records. Each document is linked so you can read it yourself. We keep copies here because the City's online minutes archive often limits how many visitors can get in.

  • Dec. 17, 2019. City Manager Keith Wright told the council he had served on the Pineywoods board for 18 years and was stepping down. He said the board had asked for someone with an engineering background. The council unanimously appointed City Engineer Kevin Gee. (Council minutes, Dec. 17, 2019, item 12)
  • Dec. 6, 2022. On its consent agenda, with no discussion recorded, the council reappointed Mr. Gee, by then city manager. (Council minutes, Dec. 6, 2022, consent item 6)
  • Same meeting. Mr. Gee presented a 10-year renewal of the Industrial District Agreement with Verdant Industries, owner of the former Abitibi paper mill on Highway 103. A motion to postpone for more information failed 2–5, and the renewal passed 5–2. Mr. Gee signed the agreement that month. Section XVI says: "the City agrees to provide water and sewer services to the Property Owner, at in City rates, for the term of this Agreement." The minutes' summary of his presentation does not mention water service. (Council minutes, Dec. 6, 2022, item 10; 2022 Verdant Industrial District Agreement, released by the City under request CRR-2026-362)
  • Sept. 2025. While serving as a Pineywoods director, Mr. Gee sent Amp Z the City's water-availability letter. (City of Lufkin letter to Amp Z, Sept. 25, 2025, released by the City under request CRR-2026-360)
  • Dec. 2, 2025. The council reappointed him again, on its consent agenda, unanimously. The staff memo lists Mr. Gee as the presenter. Resolution No. 2025-0026 appoints "Kevin T. Gee, PE" by name, with no title, for a three-year term beginning Jan. 1, 2026. (Council packet, Dec. 2, 2025, consent item 3; the meeting's minutes are in the Dec. 16, 2025 packet)
  • Nov. 9, 2026. The council accepted Mr. Gee's resignation as city manager. The district's records still show him as board president in the City of Lufkin seat, a member since Jan. 1, 2020, with his current term ending Dec. 31, 2028. (Pineywoods board terms, 2026; pgcd.org/district-board, checked Nov. 19, 2026)

What the law says. "Directors serve three-year terms," and "a director may serve consecutive terms." If a seat becomes vacant, the body that made the appointment fills it for the rest of the term. The law that created the district does not require Lufkin's director to work for the City, and it says nothing about what happens when he leaves a City job. (Texas Special District Local Laws Code ch. 8847, §§8847.051, 8847.052, 8847.054)

What we don't know yet. Whether Mr. Gee has resigned from the Pineywoods board. ETLC asked the district in writing on Nov. 18, 2026. We will update this page when we get an answer.

Every step above was taken in open session, and the City's seat on the board is lawful. We are not alleging that anyone broke the law. We are showing the record, so the public can ask the question in front of it: who should represent Lufkin on the board that regulates our aquifer?
