Pineywoods Rule 5.15: verified facts

East Texas Legacy Coalition · Last checked Nov. 19, 2026, against the rules the district adopted Nov. 9, 2026

This page is our reference for the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District's large-well rule. Every statement below is checked against the source named under it. If we find an error, we fix it here and note the date.

The adopted rule

On Nov. 9, 2026, the district's board amended its rules. The adopted rules were posted on the district's website on Nov. 12 as "District Rules Effective November 9, 2026."

Source: pgcd.org/rules · adopted rules (PDF)

The cutbacks for big wells (Rule 5.15(a)–(c))

  • A well or group of wells permitted after March 23, 2026, for 1,500 to 4,000 acre-feet a year must have one monitoring well. Above 4,000, one more monitoring well is required for every 3,000 acre-feet a year.
  • If the average water level in the monitoring wells falls 50 feet below the starting level for three months in a row, the district must temporarily suspend or reduce the permit until the level recovers.
  • If it falls 75 feet below the starting level, the district must suspend pumping for the rest of the permit period.

Source: adopted Rule 5.15(a), (b) and (c)

The deep Wilcox exemption (Rule 5.15(d))

"If the permitted production is in the Wilcox Aquifer and all of the sands screened are below a depth of 1,500 feet, then (b) and (c), above, apply only when the total permitted production for the entity is 4,000 acre-feet/year or more. The monitoring wells required in (a) above apply to these wells."

In plain terms: Wilcox wells screened entirely below 1,500 feet don't have to cut back at 50 feet or shut off at 75 feet, as long as the permit holder's total stays under 4,000 acre-feet a year. They still need monitoring wells.

The rule says nothing about water quality. The exemption depends only on depth and volume. The district's own rules define brackish water as groundwater with at least 3,000 milligrams per liter of dissolved solids, and Rule 5.15 does not use that term.

Source: adopted Rule 5.15(d) and Rule 1.1(aa)

Other parts of the Nov. 9 rules

  • City water utilities are still exempt. "This rule does not apply to a retail public water utility if the water is used to provide for its service needs within its service area within the District." (Rule 5.15(i))
  • Data centers and power plants need monitoring wells. Permits for power generation and data or computing centers require monitoring wells with automated equipment that reports water levels directly to the district. (Rule 5.16)

The vote

The district's general manager confirmed in writing that the board adopted the amendments as written in the draft, and that "the vote was unanimous, minus Kevin Gee, who was not present for the meeting."

Source: email from General Manager Wil Blough to ETLC, Nov. 10, 2026

The Pitman Family Farms applications

At the Nov. 9 meeting, Pitman Family Farms' presentation described wells targeting 1,900 to 2,300 feet and a request of 3,363 acre-feet a year.

Source: audio of the Nov. 9, 2026 meeting, provided by Protect Our Piney Woods (protectourpineywoods.com). See our Pitman drawdown map.

Who sits on the board

  • The district is governed by seven appointed directors who serve three-year terms. The Lufkin City Council appoints one of them.
  • The adopted rules, and the district's board page as of Nov. 18, 2026, still list Kevin Gee as board president, in the City of Lufkin seat. The Lufkin City Council accepted his resignation as city manager on Nov. 9, 2026.

Sources: Texas Special District Local Laws Code ch. 8847; H.B. 2572 (2001); pgcd.org/district-board; adopted rules cover page
