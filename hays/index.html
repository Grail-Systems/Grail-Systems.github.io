Hays County Watch

Between Austin and San Antonio, in a multiyear drought over the Edwards Aquifer, Hays County residents turned out by the hundreds against data centers. In June 2026, San Marcos became the first city in Texas to ban them.

What has happened

  • Jan 20, 2026

    County orders a water study

    Its first comprehensive water study since 2011.

  • Feb 17-18, 2026

    San Marcos denies a 200-acre data center

    The vote was 5-2 after an overflow crowd.

  • Feb 2026

    County tables a 30-day pause

    County attorneys warned it would almost certainly bring a lawsuit.

  • June 12, 2026

    County refuses an aquifer-zone deal

    Denied an agreement with a 340-acre development over the Edwards Aquifer recharge zone.

  • June 2026

    San Marcos bans data centers

    Voted 4-3 to prohibit them in every zoning district, the first Texas city to do so. A state senator vowed a legal challenge.

  • June 23, 2026

    County passes a symbolic pause

    A non-binding resolution pausing consideration of high-water-use developments, including data centers.

Lessons for other counties

  • Cities can do what counties can't

    Zoning power let San Marcos ban data centers outright. Find out whether a site is inside a city or its extraterritorial jurisdiction.

  • A ban is not a moratorium

    San Marcos changed its zoning rules rather than pausing permits, which it argues puts it on firmer legal ground.

  • Study your water

    A county without zoning power can still commission a water study, and the facts it finds shape every decision after.

  • Show up

    Hundreds of residents at city hall turned a rezoning vote.
