Hood County Watch

A rural county of about 62,000 people southwest of Fort Worth, with an operating bitcoin mine its neighbors have taken to court, and eight more data center proposals stacked behind it.

Compiled by Grail Systems from news reports and court filings. Checked Oct 31, 2026. Corrections welcome.

Hood County has the sixth most planned data centers of any Texas county, and ranks third per square mile (Texas Tribune, June 2026).

The projects

    MARA bitcoin mine, Granbury

    Runs next to the Wolf Hollow II gas plant. Neighbors report constant hum, vibration and health effects. Several nuisance suits are pending, including an Earthjustice-backed state case and a federal suit by nine property owners filed May 1, 2026. A county-funded study found about 60 dB near the site and 35 to 53 dB in neighborhoods.

What happened at the courthouse

  • Jan 13, 2026

    Comanche Circle conditionally approved

    After about two hours of public opposition.

  • Feb 10, 2026

    Moratorium fails, 3 to 2

    Late in an eight-hour meeting, the county attorney revealed a letter from a state senator warning the county lacked power to pass a moratorium. The senator also asked the Attorney General to investigate similar county efforts.

  • Feb 24, 2026

    Second moratorium attempt fails

    Commissioner Dave Eagle argued Hood County has special authority under a 1999 state law to protect the Lake Granbury and Brazos River watershed.

  • March 2026

    Stricter development rules adopted, unanimously

    New requirements for large industrial projects, including data centers.

  • Spring 2026

    Sued twice by developers

    After the court rejected one concept plan for lacking information such as its water source, and tabled a vote on another.

Lessons for other counties

  • Look for your own special law

    Hood County's 1999 watershed law (Local Government Code, Chapter 231, Subchapter K) gives it development powers most counties lack. Check whether your county has one, or ask your legislator for one protecting your lake or aquifer.

  • Demand the water source in writing

    Hood rejected a plan that didn't say where its water would come from. Every county can ask that question.

  • Count the permanent jobs

    One Hood County concept plan shows a peak construction workforce of 2,000 dropping to 220 permanent jobs.

  • Expect pressure from Austin

    Moratoriums drew a state senator's warning here and a $100 million lawsuit in Hill County. Contract terms and development rules are safer ground. See The Deep East Texas Standard.
