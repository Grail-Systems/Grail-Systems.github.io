Civic leadership pipeline · City of Lufkin

Every seat in Lufkin, and how regular folks get one.

A handful of names show up on board after board in this town. Not because nobody else could do it, but because most folks never hear when a seat opens. This page fixes that.

Updated Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 · Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

You don't have to know everything.

Nobody walks in knowing it all. Every member of a Lufkin board or commission has to take the state's free open-government training on the Open Meetings Act and the Public Information Act within 90 days of being appointed. You learn the job by doing it. What it takes is caring about this town and showing up.

Who can run for City Council

  • A U.S. citizen, 18 or older.
  • A qualified voter of the City of Lufkin for at least six months when you file.
  • A resident of Texas and of the City for at least one year before the election.
  • No final felony conviction (unless pardoned or released from the resulting disabilities), and not declared mentally incapacitated by a court.

No degree. No experience in office. No party.

How to file: Council is expected to call the May 1, 2027 election at its first meeting in January. Candidate packets will be posted on the City's election page when they're ready. Questions go to the City Secretary, (936) 633-0244.

Why this matters: in 2026, nobody ran against the Ward 5 and Ward 6 councilmembers. The City Secretary certified both unopposed on Feb. 25, 2026, and the City canceled that election (Ordinance No. 5154). Voters never got a choice. One neighbor filing would have changed that.

Boards and commissions

These are appointed seats. City Council picks the members, once a year or when a seat opens up. Most need you to live in Lufkin; some ask for more, listed below. Seats marked "end Dec. 1, 2026" come up for appointment this winter.

To apply or ask about an opening: City Secretary, (936) 633-0244, citysecretary@cityoflufkin.com.

Lufkin's seat on the groundwater board

Lufkin's City Council appoints one director to the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District, the board that writes the rules and permits for wells in our area. The City says Council will take up the appointment at its second meeting in December or its first meeting in January, after the incoming interim City Manager starts. The groundwater board next meets in February.
