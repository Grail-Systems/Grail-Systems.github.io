Civic leadership pipeline · City of Lufkin

Every seat in Lufkin, and how regular folks get one.

A handful of names show up on board after board in this town. Not because nobody else could do it, but because most folks never hear when a seat opens, or figure they're not qualified. You probably are. Here's what each seat really does, and how they train you.

Updated Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 · Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

You don't have to know everything.

Nobody walks in knowing it all. Here's what you need to know going in:

  • The state trains you. Every council member and every board member takes the Texas Attorney General's free open-government training (the Open Meetings Act and the Public Information Act) within 90 days. It's a short video course you can watch online.
  • The City gives board members a handbook that walks you through how meetings work.
  • Newly elected council members can go to an orientation run by the Texas Municipal League, made for people in their first term.
  • Staff does the day-to-day work. Lufkin runs on the council-manager system: the council sets direction and makes decisions, and the City Manager and staff carry them out.

What it really takes: caring about this town, reading what's in front of you, asking questions, and showing up.

City Council: elected seats

Mayor and City Council

What you'd actually doThe Mayor and six ward councilmembers make the City's big decisions together, by vote, in public meetings. That means passing city ordinances (local laws), adopting the City's budget and tax rate, hiring the City Manager, and appointing people to the boards below. Before each meeting you get an agenda packet; you read it, ask staff questions, listen to residents, and vote.
Training you getThe state's free open-government training within 90 days of taking office, plus the Texas Municipal League's orientation for newly elected city officials.
Who can run
  • A U.S. citizen, 18 or older.
  • A qualified voter of the City of Lufkin for at least six months when you file.
  • A resident of Texas and of the City for at least one year before the election.
  • No final felony conviction (unless pardoned or released from the resulting disabilities), and not declared mentally incapacitated by a court.
No degree. No experience in office. No party.
TermThree years.
How to fileCouncil is expected to call the May 1, 2027 election at its first meeting in January. Candidate packets will be posted on the City's election page when they're ready. Questions go to the City Secretary, (936) 633-0244.

Boards and commissions: appointed seats

You don't run for these. You apply, and City Council picks the members, once a year or when a seat opens. Every board member takes the state's free open-government training within 90 days and gets the City's member handbook. Seats marked "end Dec. 1, 2026" come up for appointment this winter.

To apply or ask about an opening: City Secretary, (936) 633-0244, citysecretary@cityoflufkin.com.

Planning & Zoning Commission

What you'd actually doWhen someone wants to rezone land, get a special use permit or split property into lots, it comes to you. You read the staff report, hear from the applicant and the neighbors at a public hearing, then vote to decide or recommend to Council. If something is getting built near you, it likely came through here.
How oftenMeets as needed, in open meetings.
Who can serveLufkin residents who own real estate, pay taxes and are registered to vote in the City. 3-year terms, no term limits.

Zoning Board of Adjustment & Appeals 1 seat open now

What you'd actually doWhen a property owner says the City applied its zoning rules wrong, this board hears the appeal. It works a little like a small court: you hear both sides, look at the facts, and vote. Alternates step in when a regular member can't make it, which makes an alternate seat a good way to learn.
How oftenMeets as needed, in open meetings.
Who can serveLufkin residents who own property, pay taxes and are registered to vote in the City. 2-year terms, no term limits.

4-B Economic Development Board

What you'd actually doDecide which economic development projects get money and incentives under state law, look over tax abatements, and send recommendations to Council. When a new company wants a deal to come to town, this board is where it gets worked out.
How oftenMeets as needed, in open meetings.
Who can serveLufkin residents. At least 4 of the 7 directors can't be City officers, employees or council members, so most seats are meant for regular citizens. 2-year terms, no term limits.

Lufkin's seat on the groundwater board

Lufkin's City Council appoints one director to the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District, the board that writes the rules and permits for wells in our area. The City says Council will take up the appointment at its second meeting in December or its first meeting in January, after the incoming interim City Manager starts. The groundwater board next meets in February.
