How Cedar Creek Lake won

In 2026, a Kansas company planned a data center beside Cedar Creek Lake that could draw up to 5 million gallons a day. By July, it was gone. This is how Henderson County did it, and why the fight isn't over.

Compiled by Grail Systems from news reports and the Governor's office. Checked Oct 31, 2026. Corrections welcome.

The project

  • Withdrawn July 23, 2026Reporting

    Diode Ventures, Cedar Creek Lake

    More than 700 acres near the reservoir, in the middle of a developed subdivision with hundreds of homes. Up to 5 million gallons a day from Cedar Creek Lake by year five, with a 12-year buildout. The lake supplies most of Fort Worth's drinking water.

How it was stopped

  • Early 2026

    Residents organized fast

    Volunteers formed Save Cedar Creek Lake. Thousands signed a petition demanding the project be canceled and asking lawmakers for strong data center rules.

  • 2026

    The county asked Austin for authority

    Henderson County joined other counties in a resolution asking the Legislature for power to regulate data centers.

  • May 21, 2026

    The water district hit pause

    The West Cedar Creek Municipal Utility District suspended new "high-intensity" water service commitments. Data center development in the county halted.

  • July 2026

    The Governor set a standard

    He called for closed-loop cooling, yearly water and power reporting, grid connections that lower household bills, and an end to unnecessary incentives.

  • July 23, 2026

    Diode withdrew

    The company told county officials the project did not meet the Governor's directives or community expectations.

Why it isn't over

County Commissioner Wendy Spivey has warned that the landowners are still looking to sell or use the land, and another data center could come. Watch the appraisal district for a new buyer, state TDLR building filings, and water district agendas.

Lessons for other counties

  • Find the water provider

    A county may not be able to say no, but the utility selling the water often can. Ask whether your water district, city or utility will pause large new commitments.

  • Recruit the people downstream

    Cedar Creek Lake serves Fort Worth. When a lake or aquifer feeds a bigger city, that city is an ally.

  • Tie your asks to the Governor's

    The project fell when it couldn't meet the state's own standard. See The Deep East Texas Standard.

  • Move fast and stay organized

    A volunteer group, a petition and packed meetings, all within months.
