Newton County Watch

On the Sabine River at Bon Wier, the old L-P Plywood mill site was supposed to become a $1.7 billion biorefinery, backed by more than $150 million in state and local incentives. It was never built. Now the owner is marketing the same land to data centers, with talk of on-site nuclear and gas power.

The projects

  • DisputedReporting

    Bon Wier Innovation Park

    Owned by USA BioEnergy of Scottsdale, Arizona, which calls the rail-served site, in a federal opportunity zone, Innovation Park. It advertises behind-the-meter power options including nuclear and gas. A July 31, 2026 release said the owner had entered a purchase agreement with Clean Holding Company for an East Texas Energy Campus; a tracker lists it at 1.8 GW. CEO Nick Andrews then told KJAS that no sale or deal had been made, only a letter of support. Judge Ronnie Cochran says he wrote a letter of recommendation and nothing more.

What has happened

  • 2022

    Biorefinery announced

    USA BioEnergy picked Bon Wier for a $1.7B plant to turn wood waste into fuels, citing more than $150M in state and local incentives. The Governor welcomed it.

  • 2023

    Southwest Airlines deal

    A 20-year agreement for up to 680 million gallons of sustainable aviation fuel.

  • Oct 2025

    Nuclear partner named

    Applied Atomics was announced to supply small nuclear power, and the chairman said data centers were interested in the site.

  • Late 2025

    Target completion passes

    The biorefinery has not been built.

  • July 31, 2026

    Data center deal announced

    A release said the owner entered a purchase agreement with Clean Holding Company.

  • Aug 2026

    Deal denied

    The CEO told KJAS no sale or deal was made. The county judge said he only wrote a letter of recommendation.

Lessons for other counties

  • Follow the incentives

    When a project changes from one industry to another, ask what happens to the incentives granted for the first one, and whether clawbacks apply.

  • Old mill sites are targets

    Bon Wier, like Lufkin's old paper mill, has rail, power lines and industrial history. Developers see that as a head start.

  • Letters of support matter

    A judge's letter can be quoted in a press release as an endorsement. Ask officials what they have signed, and post it publicly.

  • Watch the river

    The site sits on the Sabine River. Ask where any water would come from, and where wastewater would go.
