Ask for the records yourself

Good to know before you send it

  1. Send it to the official address. Use the agency’s public information email, online portal, mail or front desk. Some agencies won’t act on a request sent to a staff member’s own inbox.
  2. You don’t have to say why. The agency can’t ask what you want the records for. It can ask you to clarify or narrow the request.
  3. They’re on a clock. If records can’t be produced within 10 business days, the agency has to tell you in writing when they will be. If it wants to withhold something, it generally has to ask the Attorney General within 10 business days.
  4. Watch for a cost estimate. If charges will top $40, the agency has to send an itemized estimate first. You then have 10 business days to accept it, narrow the request or ask for a waiver, or the request counts as withdrawn.
  5. Keep a copy of what you sent and the date you sent it.
Sources: Texas Government Code §552.221(d), §552.222, §552.2615, §552.301. This is general information, not legal advice. The Attorney General’s Open Government Hotline answers questions for free: 512-478-6736.
