Speak at public meetings

Three minutes from a neighbor can change a room. You don't need to be an expert, just from here.

  • Sign up before it starts

    Most boards use a sign-in sheet or card at the door. Some want you signed up ahead of time, so check the agenda or call the office.

  • Speak for yourself

    Some boards allow only one speaker per group and won't let people give their time to someone else. Sign up as a resident or landowner, in your own name.

  • One point, one ask

    Say your name and where you live, make one point, and end with exactly what you want the board to do. Bring a printed copy to leave for the record.

  • Can't make it?

    Written comments usually go into the record too. Send them before the deadline on the meeting notice.

Need something to hand out? Print the one-page handout.

Request public records

Anybody can ask a Texas government body for its records. It's one of the strongest tools you have, and it costs next to nothing to ask.

  • Ask in writing

    Email the agency's public information officer. Name the records you want and the dates they cover.

  • Know the clock

    Under the Texas Public Information Act, the agency must release records promptly. If it can't within 10 business days, it has to tell you in writing when they'll be ready.

  • Ask about cost first

    If charges will be more than $40, the agency has to send you an itemized estimate before it fills the request.

  • Use the builder

    Your county page has a free request letter you can copy, like the one on Angelina County Watch.

Filed one? Tell us at contact@grailsystems.io so we can help track it. See every request we've filed for examples.

Send us what you find

Agendas, permits, news stories, photos, a sign going up on a road you drive every day. If it might matter, send it.

Include if you can: your county, what it is, where you found it, and the date.

Email a tip

Spread the word

Most people still haven't heard. Share your county's page, the statewide map or the handout wherever your neighbors are: church groups, school pages, community boards. Real links and records do more than anger.

Talk with neighbors

One honest conversation beats a hundred posts. Start with a question: "Have you heard what they're planning out here?" Leave them a printed handout and your county page.
