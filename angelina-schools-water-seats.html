Civic leadership pipeline · Angelina County

School boards, water boards, and the seats nobody runs for.

The people who set your school taxes, hire your superintendent and set your water rates are your neighbors. Most of these seats are unpaid, most come with training, and a lot of them get filled with nobody running against anybody.

Updated Monday, Nov. 23, 2026 · Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

May 2026: 11 seats, one name each.

On the May 2, 2026 ballot in Angelina County, these school and college seats had only one candidate, so there was no race:

  • Angelina College: all 4 trustee seats up.
  • Lufkin ISD: both trustee seats up.
  • Central ISD: both trustee seats up.
  • Hudson ISD: Positions 1 and 5.
  • Huntington ISD: Position 6.

Where neighbors did file, voters got a real choice: Diboll ISD had 5 candidates for 3 seats, Hudson ISD Position 4 was contested, and Huntington ISD had races for Positions 1 and 7. One person filing is all it takes.

School boards

School Board Trustee (every school district)

What you'd actually doThe school board oversees how the district is run and evaluates the superintendent. You and the other trustees adopt the budget, set the school tax rate, decide whether to ask voters for a bond, and set district policy. You don't run the schools day to day; the superintendent and staff do. Your job is to ask questions, listen to parents and teachers, and vote in public meetings.
Training you getThe state lays it out step by step. Much of it comes through the regional education service center or the Texas Education Agency's own online courses:
  • A local orientation from your own district and a 3-hour orientation to the Texas Education Code, both within 120 days.
  • Training on reading student results and setting goals, school safety, parental rights, and spotting and reporting child abuse and trafficking.
  • A yearly team-building session with the superintendent, plus continuing education hours each year.
  • The state's free online Open Meetings Act and Public Information Act courses within 90 days.
You can start some of this training up to a year before you're elected, so you know what you're walking into.
Who can run
  • A U.S. citizen, 18 or older, and a registered voter.
  • A Texas resident for 12 months and a resident of the school district for 6 months.
  • No final felony conviction (unless pardoned or released from the resulting disabilities).
You don't need a degree, a child in school, or a background in education.
TermThree years in Lufkin, Hudson, Central and Diboll ISDs. Trustees serve without pay.
How to filePick up a candidate packet from the school district's administration office during the filing period.

Angelina College

Angelina College Board of Trustees

What you'd actually doGovern the college: set policy and goals that fit the college's mission, adopt the budget and tax rate, and hire and evaluate the college president. The college's taxing district is all of Angelina County, so every voter in the county elects these trustees.
Training you getThe state's free online Open Meetings Act and Public Information Act courses.
Who can runA registered voter who lives in Angelina County, under the same basic state rules as other local offices.
TermSix years. Elections are in May of even-numbered years.
Seats7 trustees · all 4 seats on the 2026 ballot had one name · next election May 2028

Water boards

Who you can vote for, or run for, depends on who sends you your water bill. The public water systems serving Angelina County include Angelina WSC, Central WCID of Angelina County, City of Diboll, Four Way SUD, Hudson WSC, City of Huntington, City of Lufkin, M&M WSC, Pollok-Redtown WSC, Redland WSC, Woodlawn Water Utilities (formerly Woodlawn WSC, now run by the Angelina & Neches River Authority), and City of Zavalla. If a city sends your bill, the city council is your water board.

Water Supply Corporation director (the "WSC" on your bill)

What you'd actually doA water supply corporation is a nonprofit owned by its members, the people it serves. The elected board is responsible for running it, and the board sets the water rates. If 10 percent of the affected customers object, they can ask the state's Public Utility Commission to review a rate change.
Training you getWater supply corporations have to hold open meetings like any local government, so directors take the state's free online Open Meetings Act course.
Who can serveAny member 18 or older. You can't serve if a court has found you mentally incapacitated, or with a final felony conviction (unless pardoned or released from the resulting disabilities).
How to runFile a candidate application with the corporation at least 45 days before the annual meeting: the seat you want, your consent to serve, a short bio and a statement that you qualify. Very large corporations (1,500 or more members) also need a petition signed by 20 members. Members can vote at the meeting or by mail.
WhenThe annual meeting has to happen between Jan. 1 and May 1. The corporation has to tell members about the filing deadline at least 30 days ahead, so watch your bill and mail.

Special Utility District director (Four Way SUD)

What you'd actually doSame kind of job as a water supply corporation board, but the district is a unit of local government and its directors are elected in public elections, run by the county.
Training you getThe state's free online Open Meetings Act course.
Who can serve18 or older, a Texas resident, and one of these: you own taxable land in the district, you use the district's water, or you're a qualified voter in the district.
TermNo more than three years.
NoteFour Way SUD's 2024 election was canceled because every candidate was unopposed.

Water district director (WCIDs and fresh water supply districts)

What you'd actually doThese districts are local governments that provide water service in a set area. The board makes the district's decisions in public meetings. In Angelina County this includes Central WCID of Angelina County, Angelina County WCID No. 4, Idlewood WCID, and Angelina County Fresh Water Supply District No. 1, which the Angelina & Neches River Authority manages under contract.
Training you getThe state's free online Open Meetings Act course.
Who can serveDepends on the type of district. For a fresh water supply district, you must be a registered voter of the district, or a Texas resident who owns taxable property in it.
TermGenerally four years, staggered, with elections in May of even-numbered years.
NoteIdlewood WCID canceled its May 2026 election. WCID No. 4 posted a filing notice for 2026.

Appointed water seats

Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District

What you'd actually doWrite the rules and issue the permits for water wells in Angelina and Nacogdoches counties, which means deciding how much can be pumped from the aquifer under our feet.
Who picks the boardSeven appointed directors. The Angelina County Commissioners Court appoints two (one for rural water and small municipal water supply, one for large industrial groundwater users). Nacogdoches County appoints two. Lufkin's City Council and Nacogdoches's City Council appoint one each. The two counties jointly appoint one for forestry, agricultural or landowner interests.
How to step upTell your commissioner or council member you want the seat, and show up when the appointment is on the agenda. Lufkin's seat comes up in December or January.

Angelina & Neches River Authority

What you'd actually doHelp govern the river authority that runs water and sewer utilities, a compost facility and water-quality work across 17 counties in the Neches River basin.
Training you getUnder a 2025 state law, new directors must finish training on the authority's law, budget, audits, open meetings, public information and ethics before they can vote.
Who picks the boardNine directors appointed by the Governor. Terms are now four years, with four or five ending every two years. You apply through the Governor's appointments office.
